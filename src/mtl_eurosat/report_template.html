Does a reconstruction task make a satellite classifier robust to a new sensor?

A course project claimed that training one encoder to classify and reconstruct satellite images makes it generalise better. Re-run over $n_seeds seeds and scored on aerial images from a different sensor, the claim does not hold: the auxiliary loss leaves out-of-distribution accuracy where it was, and single runs were too noisy to say anything at all.

The question

Train on Sentinel-2 patches of forest and residential land (EuroSAT), deploy on Google Earth aerial images of the same two land uses (AID). Classification-only training can latch onto shortcuts of the training sensor; a reconstruction head forces the shared encoder to keep the whole image. Does that auxiliary task buy robustness to the sensor change? The answer decides whether an auxiliary loss is a cheap robustness fix or a distraction from collecting target-domain data.

Setup

  • Train / validate: 6,000 EuroSAT RGB patches (3,000 forest, 3,000 residential, 64×64), stratified 80/20 split drawn per seed.
  • Out of distribution: 120 AID images resized to 64×64 — 60 forest, 30 dense residential, 30 medium residential. Never used for training or checkpoint selection.
  • Models (the course notebooks' architectures, unchanged): a 24k-parameter single-task CNN; a 193k-parameter hard-sharing network (shared encoder, classification head, decoder) trained on α·CE + (1 − α)·MSE; a soft-sharing network (two branches tied by an alignment loss, masked-autoencoder reconstruction).
  • Control: the hard-sharing network with α = 1 — same architecture, reconstruction switched off — separates the effect of the auxiliary loss from the effect of an 8× larger model.
  • Protocol: 10 epochs of Adam (lr 10⁻³, batch 32), checkpoint with the lowest validation loss, $n_seeds seeds for the headline variants and $n_seeds_sweep for the rest of the α sweep. Differences are paired by seed (same split).

Every error is a forest called residential

All models recognise AID residential scenes. The single-task CNN gets $cnn_forest of AID forests right, hard-sharing MTL $hard06_forest. The ranking is better than the accuracy suggests (AUROC $cnn_auroc and $hard06_auroc): many forests receive a lower residential score than the residential images, but still above the 0.5 threshold — a shift of the decision boundary as much as a failure of the features.

At 64×64, a Sentinel-2 forest is a smooth green field; a 600×600 Google Earth forest, shrunk to the same size, shows crowns, shadows and clearings. Soft sharing: a train/test mismatch, not a property of MTL

The soft-sharing network is trained only on images with 30% of their pixels masked. Scored on clean EuroSAT validation images it reaches $soft_val (vs $soft_val_masked on masked ones), and on clean AID images $soft_ood ($soft_ood_masked when masked). Its collapse in the original notebook ($nb_soft) came from scoring a masked-input model on unmasked images.

Limitations

  • The AID set is small: 60 forest images, so one image is 1.7 points of forest accuracy. Confidence intervals are across seeds and do not include the sampling error of the test set itself.
  • One architecture family, 10 epochs, no data augmentation, no tuning of α beyond the sweep, the soft-sharing weights as set in the notebook.
  • Training ran on an Apple GPU (MPS), which is not bit-for-bit deterministic; re-runs reproduce the distributions, not every digit.
  • Untested here and more likely to help: colour/scale augmentation, a handful of labelled AID images, domain adaptation.
