Does a reconstruction task make a satellite classifier robust to a new sensor?

A course project claimed that training one encoder to classify and reconstruct satellite images makes it generalise better. Re-run over $n_seeds seeds and scored on aerial images from a different sensor, the claim does not hold for accuracy: hard-sharing MTL lands where a single-task CNN already was, and soft sharing's accuracy gain is matched by input masking alone. Only soft sharing's ranking (AUROC) improves, for reasons this design cannot isolate.

The question

Train on Sentinel-2 patches of forest and residential land (EuroSAT), deploy on Google Earth aerial images of the same two land uses (AID). Classification-only training can latch onto shortcuts of the training sensor; a reconstruction head forces the shared encoder to keep the whole image. Does that auxiliary task buy robustness to the sensor change? The answer decides whether an auxiliary loss is a cheap robustness fix or a distraction from augmentation and target-domain labels.

Setup

  • Train / validate: 6,000 EuroSAT RGB patches (3,000 forest, 3,000 residential, 64×64), stratified 80/20 split drawn per seed.
  • Out of distribution: 120 AID images resized to 64×64: 60 forest, 30 dense residential, 30 medium residential. Never used for training or checkpoint selection.
  • Models (the course notebooks' architectures): a $params_cnn-parameter single-task CNN; a $params_hard-parameter hard-sharing network (shared encoder, classification head, decoder) trained on α·CE + (1 − α)·MSE; a $params_soft-parameter soft-sharing network (two branches tied by an alignment loss, masked-autoencoder reconstruction on inputs with 30% of pixels zeroed).
  • Controls: the hard-sharing network with α = 1 (same architecture, reconstruction switched off) separates the auxiliary loss from the different architecture; the single-task CNN trained on the same 30%-masked inputs separates soft sharing from its input masking.
  • Protocol: 10 epochs of Adam (lr 10⁻³, batch 32), checkpoint with the lowest validation loss, $n_seeds seeds for the headline variants and $n_seeds_sweep for the rest of the α sweep. Differences are paired by seed (same split) with a t interval.

Where the errors are

The single-task CNN gets every AID residential group at least $cnn_res_min right but only $cnn_forest of AID forests. Hard-sharing MTL recovers forests ($hard06_forest) and pays for them on medium-density residential scenes ($hard06_medium): it trades one error for another rather than seeing the new sensor better. AUROC ($cnn_auroc CNN, $hard06_auroc MTL) is well above what the accuracy suggests: many forests get a lower residential score than the residential images, but still above 0.5.

At 64×64, a Sentinel-2 forest is a smooth green field; a 600×600 Google Earth forest, shrunk to the same size, shows crowns, shadows and clearings. Soft sharing: the accuracy gain is the masking

The soft-sharing network only ever sees images with 30% of their pixels zeroed. Scored on clean EuroSAT images it reaches $soft_val ($soft_val_masked on masked ones) and on clean AID images $soft_ood: the notebook's collapse to $nb_soft came from scoring a masked-input model on unmasked images. Scored as trained, it reaches $soft_ood_masked on AID, $soft_vs_cnn above the single-task CNN.

The masking ablation explains that gain. The single-task CNN trained and scored on the same masked inputs reaches $cm_ood_masked ($masking against the plain CNN, $masking_p); soft sharing adds $soft_vs_cm on top ($soft_vs_cm_p). Its AUROC is higher, $soft_auroc_masked against $cm_auroc_masked ($soft_vs_cm_auroc, $soft_vs_cm_auroc_p), but with $params_ratio× more parameters the comparison cannot credit the reconstruction for it. Used as plain augmentation, masking helps most: the masked-input CNN scored on clean AID images reaches $cm_ood_clean ($masking_clean against the plain CNN, $masking_clean_p; forest $cm_forest_clean), at the price of $cm_val_clean on clean EuroSAT validation.

Limitations

  • The AID set is small: 60 forest images, so one image is 1.7 points of forest accuracy. Confidence intervals are across seeds and do not include the sampling error of the test set itself.
  • $n_comparisons paired comparisons are reported, without a multiple-testing correction. At the Bonferroni threshold (p < $bonferroni) $n_bonferroni stay significant: $bonferroni_list. Effects with p between that threshold and 0.05 are suggestive, not established.
  • One architecture family, 10 epochs, no data augmentation beyond the masking ablation, no tuning of α beyond the sweep, the soft-sharing loss weights as set in the notebook.
  • The notebooks kept model.state_dict() as their "best" checkpoint. That is a view of the live weights, so they restored the last epoch trained. The pipeline deep-copies the best checkpoint. AID is scored after every epoch for diagnosis only and never drives checkpoint selection.
  • Training ran on an Apple GPU (MPS), which is not bit-for-bit deterministic; re-runs reproduce the distributions, not every digit.
  • Untested here and more likely to help: colour and scale augmentation, a handful of labelled AID images, domain adaptation.
